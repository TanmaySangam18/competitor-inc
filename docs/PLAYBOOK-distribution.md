# Playbook — Distribution (the axis that decides competitor.inc)

> Companion to [PLAYBOOK-path-to-10k.md](PLAYBOOK-path-to-10k.md). The honest five-factor read scored us
> **three greens** (timing, position, model) and **one red — distribution/team**. Startups in this
> category live or die on that one axis. So this is not a product playbook; it is a **distribution
> playbook**, aimed squarely at the weak spot, with the **founder present** (not walking away) and the
> **House agents carrying the scalable load.**
>
> **The founder's stance:** *I am not walking away.* You are the **trust champion** for launch + the first
> ~30 days — not the operator. The agents run the machine; you do the few things that can't be automated.
>
> **Beachhead = first-time / student & new-grad founders** (the WHO, not a vertical). Channels skew to where
> they gather: campus orgs, **NU IDEA/Sherman**, student & indie communities (IndieHackers, r/startups), and
> honesty-reveal reels framed for first-timers. (Crossing the Chasm: own the segment.)

## The playbook: three proven plays, run in sequence

Each play fixes a specific failure mode from the pre-mortem. The **sequence is the point** — skip ahead and
you pour fuel on a fire that isn't lit.

### ① Paul Graham — *Do Things That Don't Scale* (Weeks 0–2)
**Fixes:** the walk-away gap · handoff stall · "no one drove the launch."

In the beginning the **founder is the distribution engine**. You manually recruit the first 50–100 users,
one at a time. For competitor.inc:
- **Hand-recruit the beachhead** — DM burned indie hackers on X / IndieHackers / r/SaaS. Not a pitch:
  *"you got burned by a Polsia-type tool, I built the opposite, want in free?"*
- **Be in the launch thread** — reply to every Show HN / Product Hunt comment within minutes. Founder-in-
  thread beats any ad.
- **Hand-hold to the first "aha"** — personally walk early users to their first validation run; fix the
  message off their real reactions.

### ② Weinberg & Mares — *Bullseye* (from *Traction*) (Weeks 2–4)
**Fixes:** the #1 killer — "no repeatable channel."

- **Outer ring** — brainstorm ~5 channels: HN/PH launches, beachhead communities, comparison SEO
  ("Polsia alternative"), founder DMs, one tiny paid test.
- **Middle ring** — run cheap parallel tests on all of them in the same week.
- **Inner ring** — pick the **ONE** with the best cost per signup→paid, pour everything in, kill the rest.

One channel that reliably turns strangers into payers beats five that sort-of work. **That channel is the
real prize — more than the $10K.**

### ③ Sean Ellis — *The 40% PMF Test* (the gate, end of Week 4)
**Fixes:** try-but-don't-pay · pouring fuel on an unlit fire.

Before scaling spend, survey active users one question: *"How would you feel if you could no longer use
competitor.inc?"* **≥ 40% "very disappointed" → you have PMF; automate and scale** (and *now* you can earn
the walk-away). **< 40% → do not spend; fix the product or message first.**

```
Weeks 0–2   Don't-Scale   → founder recruits by hand, owns the launch
Weeks 2–4   Bullseye      → find the ONE channel
End Wk 4    40% Gate      → PMF? scale :: no PMF? fix first
Month 2     Pour fuel on the proven channel → push toward $10K
```

**The one metric the whole thing rides on: signup → paid %.** Everything above exists to move it and to
find the channel that moves it cheaply.

## Can the agents do this plan? The honest split

Phase 1 is *Do Things That Don't Scale* — and **"doesn't scale" is the definition of "an agent can't do
it"**: the moment something can be automated, it scales. So the part that needs a human is *structurally*
the part agents can't own. That's physics, not a product flaw. The trust-work is also exactly where our
brand lives — an AI mass-DMing communities is spam, gets banned, and destroys the one thing we sell.

| Playbook task | Owner | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cold 1:1 trust DMs to burned founders | 🔴 **You** | Trust is the wedge; AI mass-DMs = spam + bans + brand suicide |
| Replying in the Show HN / PH thread | 🔴 **You** | HN/PH ban AI/astroturf; agent-as-founder is dishonest (breaks our brand) |
| Judgment calls (which channel; scale vs fix) | 🔴 **You** (agents recommend) | The approval *is* the product — human-in-control is the wedge |
| Onboarding + hand-holding to first "aha" | 🟢 **Guard** | Automatable — 24/7 guidance to the first validation run |
| Drafting outreach / posts / content | 🟡 **Echo/Pitch/Quill draft → you send** | Agents write 10×; you add the human signature + hit send |
| Comparison SEO at volume | 🟢 **Quill** (you gate quality) | The compounding channel; agents produce, you approve |
| Bullseye channel tests + CAC/conversion | 🟢 **Surge + Quant** | Instrumentation + scoreboard + math = fully automatable |
| Paid ad execution within caps | 🟢 **Pitch** (approval-gated) | Agents place/optimize; you approve the spend |
| 40% PMF survey — send, collect, compute | 🟢 **Quant** | Mechanics fully automatable; you read the result |
| Trial→paid nudges, pricing clarity | 🟢 **Ledger** | Automatable conversion work |

**The trap:** trying to make agents do the trust-work anyway (mass AI DMs) to "prove autonomy." It
backfires — bans, destroyed trust. Let agents be strong where they're strong; you cover the one zone they
can't.

## The real validation: the leverage ratio

competitor.inc never promised *"the founder disappears."* It promised **AI builds and runs; the human
stays in control** (the Approval Inbox — our counter-position). So the honest validation isn't "agents do
100%." It's:

> **Can one human — doing only the irreducible trust + judgment + approval work — plus an agent crew,
> produce the output of a ~10-person team?**

That's measurable. **Track the work-share to $10K.** If the agents carry ~**80–90%** (build, run, support,
content, measurement, ad-execution, survey) and you carry the ~**10–20%** that's pure trust/judgment/
approval — **the product is validated.** If you end up doing the majority yourself, it isn't — and you'll
know honestly by month 2.

**The scoreboard:** at $10K MRR, *what % of the total work was the human?* **Lower is the win.** That single
number is the truest proof the whole thesis works — more than the revenue itself.
