# Autonomy model — supervised autonomy

## "If we wait for approval, how is it autonomous?"

Autonomy isn't all-or-nothing — it's a spectrum, and competitor.inc sits at **supervised autonomy
(human-*on*-the-loop, not human-*in*-the-loop):**

- It **autonomously does the work** — researches, builds, writes copy, runs tests, ships to staging,
  drafts campaigns and posts — with **no human in the loop**. That's the "building."
- It **only pauses for one-way doors** (Bezos's framing): actions that are *irreversible or
  reputational* — spending real money above a cap, sending outbound messages, deploying to
  production, deleting. Those are a tiny fraction of the work.

So in a typical overnight shift, **most actions auto-execute**; only the consequential few become
approvals. It *is* building on its own — it just won't gamble your money or your name without a nod.

**It's also tunable (the autonomy slider):** Autopilot runs shifts on a heartbeat automatically, and
you can raise the spend cap / narrow which action *kinds* need sign-off. Crank the caps up and it runs
nearly hands-off; the **default is "earn trust first."**

**Why this is a feature, not a limitation.** The counter-example: a reviewer gave Polsia a credit card,
clicked "surprise me," and it "instantly went full stalker mode." Unbounded autonomy is a liability.
Our approval gate is the thing that lets you *actually* leave it running.

**Playbook:** Bezos one-way / two-way doors + a trust ladder (start supervised, grant more autonomy as
it earns it).
