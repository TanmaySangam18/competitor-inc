# Landing Page Clarity & Messaging Audit — competitor.inc (2026-07-07)

_Audited the real `app/page.tsx`. Scoring scale: 0 = instantly obvious, 100 = extremely confusing (higher =
worse). Brutally honest, benchmarked against Lovable. Not preserving existing copy unless it's demonstrably
the clearest option._

## The one-line diagnosis
**The hero sells the first step (idea validation), not the product (an AI crew that validates → builds →
runs a software company). Visitors under-scope it to "another idea validator," and the ICP is never stated.**
Everything else is secondary to fixing that.

---

## Phase 1 — First impression (5 seconds)
- **What does it do?** "An AI crew validates your startup idea, live." — but the real product (build + operate a
  company) is invisible. Wrong mental model formed.
- **Who is it for?** Not stated anywhere above the fold. ("idea" implies founders; students/first-timers absent.)
- **What problem?** "Know if your idea's worth building" — only the validation slice.
- **Understand without scrolling?** Partially — you grasp "AI validates ideas," not the product.
- **Know what to click?** Yes — input + "Run validation" is obvious (a genuine strength).
- **Why different?** "It'll tell you not to build" is a real hook but understated; "orgware engineering,"
  "Revenue loop," "Governed" are jargon a visitor can't parse.
- **Why care?** Weak — if it reads as "an idea validator," a founder shrugs.

## Phase 2 — Lovable benchmark
Lovable lands and you *instantly* know it: **"Build something ✦ — Idea to app, fast."** One verb (build), one
outcome (app), one input (chat box). No jargon, no abstraction.
- **Headline:** Lovable names the outcome (an app). Ours names an activity (validate) — and the wrong-scope one.
- **Subheadline:** Lovable = "what + how." Ours = a caveat ("will tell you not to build") before the value.
- **CTA:** Lovable's CTA = the product verb ("Build"). Ours = "Run validation" (a sub-feature).
- **Language:** Lovable = plain ("app," "build"). Ours = coined/abstract ("orgware," "Revenue loop," "Governed").
- **Feature explanation:** Lovable shows the *output* (a live app). Ours shows *governance mechanics* before the
  visitor even knows what gets built.
- **Where we match/beat it:** visual simplicity, the live interactive hook, and the trust framing are strong.

**Friction points vs. Lovable:** (1) headline scope, (2) no ICP, (3) jargon, (4) no "what you end up with,"
(5) no 3-step "how it works," (6) proof boxes lead with mechanics not outcomes.

## Phase 3 — Cognitive-load audit
- **Coined jargon — "orgware engineering," "Revenue loop," "Verifiable. Governed."** → visitor: *"what do these
  mean?"* → replace with plain outcomes ("every action is logged," "judged on real revenue," "you approve every dollar").
- **6 co-equal boxes, no hierarchy** → visitor: *"which of these is the point?"* → collapse to 3 outcome-led proofs.
- **Hero scope mismatch** → visitor: *"so it's an idea checker?"* → state validate→build→run in the hero.
- **Missing "how it works"** → a novel product with no 3-step mental model forces the visitor to assemble it.
- **Missing ICP** → *"is this for me?"* → name the audience (first-time / student founders).
- **"Simulated run" disclaimer in the aha moment** → honest + necessary, but it slightly deflates the wow; keep
  it, but pair with "…then the real crew builds it for real."
- **Low overall text is good** — don't add bulk; add the *right* 20 words (product scope + ICP + 3 steps).

---

## Phase 4 — Information architecture
Current: Nav → Hero+demo → 6-box bento → single CTA → giant wordmark → footer.
**Ideal order:**
1. **Hero** — product in one line (validate→build→run) + ICP + the live demo (keep the demo; it's the aha).
2. **How it works — 3 steps** (NEW): Describe your idea → Approve the plan → The crew builds it & works the
   growth, you approve every dollar. *This is the missing keystone for a novel product.*
3. **What you get** (reframe): "a real, live product + a crew that runs the day-to-day" — show the *output*.
4. **Why trust an AI with your company** (reframe the bento, 3 outcome-led proofs): you approve everything ·
   every action logged · it'll tell you not to build.
5. **Proof/traction** (NEW slot) — real numbers/testimonials when they exist; until then, the honesty pledge
   ("we never fake a metric") as the trust anchor. *Do not fabricate.*
6. **Final CTA** + risk-reducer ("free to validate, you approve any spend").
- **Delete/shrink:** the 10vw wordmark block (vanity, pushes CTA away); "orgware" tagline (move to /about).
- **Merge:** "Revenue loop" + "Governed" + "Glass box" → one "how we keep it safe + honest" trio.

## Phase 5 — Above-the-fold
Current hero fails 3 of 5 in 5s: **what (mis-scoped), who (absent), why-different (understated).** Passes:
what-to-click, basic why-care. No social proof, no product image of the *output* (only appears after you run
the demo). **Rewrite (below).**

## Phase 7 — User psychology
- **Curiosity:** the live demo is excellent — strong pull. Keep.
- **"I understand this":** ✗ initially (scope) → ✓ after the rewrite.
- **"I trust this":** ✓ strong (glass box, your-yes, honest) — a real asset; surface it as *why AI-run is safe*.
- **"I need this":** ✗ — a validator is a nice-to-have; "an AI that builds+runs my company" is a need. The scope
  fix converts nice-to-have → need.
- **"I should try this":** ✓ (no signup, free, instant demo) — low friction is a strength.
- **Perceived effort / risk:** low + well-handled ("free," "you approve spend"). Good.

---

## Phase 8 — CTA audit
- Nav "Get started," hero "Run validation," post-verdict "Keep this crew," footer "Put a crew on your idea" —
  **four different labels for ~two actions.** Inconsistent → pick one verb.
- "Run validation" mis-frames the primary action as a sub-feature.
- No urgency/scarcity (fine pre-launch; a "founding cohort / limited spots" line would help once launching).
- **Recommend:** primary CTA verb everywhere = **"Start free"** (or "See it work" on the demo). Post-verdict =
  "Build this for real →". Keep the risk-reducer under every CTA.

## Phase 9 — Full copy rewrite
**Nav:** logo · How it works · Sign in · **[Start free]**

**Hero**
- H1: **An AI crew that builds and runs your startup.**
- Sub: **Describe your idea. competitor.inc validates it, ships a real product, and works the growth playbook —
  and nothing spends or ships without your yes. Built for first-time founders.**
- Input placeholder: "an AI resume coach for new grads" (keep) · Button: **See it work**
- Under demo verdict: "That's the 60-second validation. Say the word and the crew builds it for real →
  **[Build this →]**" + "Free to validate · you approve every dollar."

**How it works (3 steps, NEW)**
1. **Describe it.** One sentence. The crew gives you an honest demand verdict — receipts included.
2. **Approve the plan.** You're the founder. Nothing happens without your yes.
3. **The crew builds & runs it.** A real, live product — then daily growth work, drafted for your approval.

**Why it's safe to hand a company to AI (reframed bento, 3)**
- **You approve everything.** Every plan, every dollar — from the app, Slack, or Telegram.
- **Every action is logged.** See exactly what each agent did, with proof. No black box.
- **It's honest.** It'll tell you *not* to build — and it never fakes a number.

**Proof (NEW, honest):** real outcomes/quotes when they exist; until then: *"We show real receipts, never
projections. If a number isn't real, you won't see it here."*

**Final CTA:** **Start your company — free.** + "You approve anything that costs money."

**Footer:** unchanged (good).

## Phase 10 — Layout recommendations
- **Add a 3-step "how it works"** band under the hero (icons + one line each) — the single biggest clarity win.
- **Reframe the 6 boxes → 3 outcome-led proofs**; more white space, fewer competing ideas.
- **Lead each proof with the outcome, not the mechanic** (title = benefit, sub = how).
- **Shrink the 10vw wordmark** to a normal footer lockup; it currently eats a full viewport for vanity and
  buries the final CTA.
- **Show the *output*** (a screenshot of a built site / the operating dashboard) near "what you get" — visitors
  should see the end state, not just governance mechanics.
- Keep: typography, spacing, ink-on-cream restraint, the live demo panel (all strong).

## Phase 11 — Conversion optimization (prioritized)
- **[Critical]** Hero scope + ICP — visitors mis-understand the product. *(single highest-leverage fix)*
- **[Critical]** Add "how it works" 3-step — novel product needs a mental model.
- **[High]** De-jargon ("orgware/Revenue loop/Governed" → plain outcomes).
- **[High]** One consistent primary CTA verb.
- **[Medium]** Reframe bento to 3 outcome-led proofs.
- **[Medium]** Show the product's output (screenshot).
- **[Low]** Shrink vanity wordmark; move "orgware" line to /about.
- **[Low]** Add founding-cohort urgency once launching.

## Phase 12 — Final redesign (structure)
Nav (1 link + 1 CTA) → **Hero** (build+run + ICP + live demo) → **How it works (3 steps)** → **What you get**
(output screenshot) → **Why it's safe** (3 outcome proofs) → **Proof/honesty pledge** → **Final CTA** → footer.
Every section answers one question in order: *what is it → how does it work → what do I get → why trust it →
proof → act.*

## Final report
- **Overall confusion (before): ~48/100** (clean design, mis-scoped messaging).
- **Overall confusion (after, projected): ~22/100** (Lovable-tier: product clear in 3–5s).
- **Estimated clarity increase:** ~55%. **Cognitive-load reduction:** ~40%. **Conversion-potential lift:**
  meaningful (scope fix converts "nice-to-have" → "need"); treat as a hypothesis to A/B, not a guarantee.

**Top 10 messaging problems:** (1) hero scopes to validation not the product; (2) no ICP; (3) "orgware"
jargon; (4) "Revenue loop" abstract; (5) "Verifiable. Governed." corporate; (6) 4 CTA labels for 2 actions;
(7) leads with a caveat before value; (8) "Run validation" names a sub-feature; (9) no "what you get"
statement; (10) no "how it works."

**Top 10 UX problems:** (1) no 3-step model; (2) 6 co-equal boxes, no hierarchy; (3) product output only
appears after running the demo; (4) vanity wordmark buries the CTA; (5) proof leads with mechanics; (6) no
visible ICP path (the /nu page is hidden); (7) no persistent "how it works" nav until footer; (8) demo
disclaimer slightly deflates the aha; (9) no output screenshot; (10) inconsistent CTA styling.

**Top 10 design problems:** (1) wordmark block scale; (2) bento density vs. narrative; (3) benefit-vs-mechanic
titling; (4) no product imagery; (5) proof boxes visually equal weight; (6) whitespace could separate ideas
more; (7) icon set is generic; (8) no visual "step" affordance; (9) verdict panel styling could feel more
"product"; (10) footer is the only depth path (fine, but "how it works" should be in the nav).

**Top 10 conversion problems:** (1) mis-scoped value prop; (2) missing mental model; (3) jargon friction;
(4) inconsistent CTAs; (5) no output preview; (6) no social proof slot; (7) caveat-before-value ordering;
(8) buried final CTA; (9) no ICP self-identification; (10) no (honest) urgency once launching.

**Top 10 trust problems (note: trust is actually a relative strength):** (1) no real social proof yet
(pre-launch — don't fake); (2) "simulated run" wording; (3) coined jargon can read as hype; (4) claims
("runs itself") risk overclaiming autonomy — keep honest; (5) no named founder/face; (6) no security/privacy
cue above fold; (7) no "who's behind this"; (8) no real logos/press; (9) verdict is simulated on the landing;
(10) "provably" without a visible proof link. *Fixes: honesty pledge, real receipts when they exist, a face/story.*

**Top 25 improvements by impact:** hero scope → 3-step how-it-works → ICP line → de-jargon → consistent CTA →
output screenshot → reframe bento to 3 → honesty/proof slot → shrink wordmark → "build this for real" post-verdict
→ nav "how it works" → benefit-led titles → risk-reducer under every CTA → move orgware to /about → founder
face/story → security cue → founding-cohort urgency (at launch) → social proof (when real) → whitespace between
ideas → output-first over mechanics → verdict panel polish → generic-icon upgrade → /nu ICP path surfaced →
demo-disclaimer rewording → A/B the new hero.

**Verdict:** the design is already professional and the trust framing is a genuine moat — but the page is
selling a **validator** when the product is an **AI that builds and runs your company.** Fix the hero scope +
add a 3-step model + de-jargon, and this moves from "somewhat confusing" to Lovable-tier clear.
