# Growth model — evidence-based, scenario-based, honestly bounded

> Every figure is tagged **[benchmark]** (external data, sourced) or **[assumption]** (a reasoned input,
> with a range). We have **zero first-party data** today, so this is a *model*, not a measurement. The
> whole point of Blocks 1 & 3 (waitlist capture + real demand test) is to replace these assumptions with
> our own measured numbers within ~2–4 weeks — at which point the probabilities below tighten sharply.

## 0 · The base rate you must internalize
- **70% of micro-SaaS never exceed $1,000/mo** in revenue. [benchmark — Indie Hackers / MicroConf surveys]
- **Median time to $10K MRR for bootstrapped SaaS is 12–18 months.** Time to first dollar ~3mo; to $1K MRR ~12–18mo. [benchmark — Indie Hackers / MicroConf]
- For a founder **without an existing audience**, realistic revenue is ~**$500–$2K (mo 1), $2K–$10K (mo 3), $5K–$25K (mo 6), $10K–$50K (mo 12)** — and **full-time founders progress 3–4× faster** than part-time. [benchmark — MicroConf/solo-founder data]

**Implication:** "$10K MRR within ~1 month from a cold start" sits in the **top few percent** of outcomes. This model is built to *maximize the probability*, not to pretend the base rate doesn't exist.

## 1 · Unit economics — what "$10K MRR" actually requires
- Operator **$39/mo** → $10,000 / 39 = **~257 net active paying subscribers**.
- Founding **$99 one-time × 150 = $14,850 cash** — but this is **NOT MRR** (one-time). It funds runway; recurring revenue must come from Operator (or an annual/Studio tier).
- **ARPU is a direct divisor:** blended $50 → 200 subs; $60 → 167 subs. Raising ARPU (annual prepay, a higher "Studio" tier) is the cheapest way to cut the customer count needed.

## 4 · Probability of milestones (priors; wide confidence intervals)

| Milestone | Probability | Why (reasoning) |
|---|---|---|
| $10K MRR in **30 days** | **2–5%** | Benchmark mo-1 = $500–2K; needs an extreme viral hit + instant top-quartile conversion |
| $10K MRR in **90 days** | **10–20%** | Requires the Aggressive scenario (a channel hits); possible, not likely |
| $10K MRR in **6 months** | **25–40%** | Benchmark mo-6 = $5–25K; in range with a working channel + mid-benchmark conversion + full-time work |
| $10K MRR in **12 months** | **35–55%** | Median time is 12–18mo; full-time is 3–4× faster — **but** 70%-never-pass-$1K caps the upside |
| **2,000 waitlist** (pre-launch) | **25–45%** | A free action converts higher (~10–30% of interested visitors); the campus/niche channel is the realistic engine |

**Confidence: LOW→MODERATE.** These are priors from comparables, not measurements. Each tightens (up or down) the moment our own funnel data lands. Treat them as a starting distribution to update, not a forecast to trust.

## 5 · Sensitivity — the variables with the biggest impact (in order)
1. **Finding ONE working acquisition channel** (the visitor count): a 13× swing (3K→40K) in the model — the single largest, highest-variance lever. [framework — Bullseye/Traction: most growth comes from *one* channel; concentrate, don't spray.]
2. **Free→paid conversion** (2%→8% = 4× swing): move from median to top-quartile via onboarding/activation. [benchmark — ProfitWell: ACV >$50 + strong onboarding lifts conversion 40–60%.]
3. **ARPU / pricing** (direct divisor): annual prepay + a higher tier cut the customer count needed by 20–35%.
4. **Retention / churn** (caps *net* MRR): at 5% monthly churn you shed ~14% over 90 days and must out-add it; NRR >100% compounds in your favor.

## 6 · Raising the probability toward the realistic ceiling
- **The revenue milestone cannot honestly be pushed to 90%+ in a short window** — it depends on market response we don't control. Anyone claiming otherwise is guessing.
- **What CAN reach ~90%+ are the controllable execution milestones:** ship + instrument + run **≥10 real demand tests** + collect first-party conversion data + gather **≥40 Sean-Ellis survey responses**. These are ~90%+ *if we simply do them consistently* — and they're what *de-risk* the revenue bet.
- **Highest realistic ceiling for $10K MRR @12mo ≈ 50–60%**, and only under ALL of: (a) full-time, consistent execution; (b) **one validated channel with CAC < LTV**; (c) free→paid **≥ median (4%)**, ideally top-quartile; (d) churn **<5%/mo**; (e) **Sean-Ellis ≥40%** "very disappointed" (the PMF threshold) — and a fast pivot if it isn't. Miss (b) or (e) and the ceiling drops sharply.

## 8 · ChatOps ("InkBox" — internal codename only) + free API + the advertising reality
- **Clarification (honest):** "InkBox" is **our internal codename** for the *text-your-agents / approve-from-your-phone* feature — **not an external company**. There's no external "InkBox" to license an API from or to charge for ads. (If a specific external product was meant, flag it and I'll evaluate that instead.)
- **The free API to power it: Telegram Bot API — completely free, unlimited messages, instant setup (no Meta verification).** [benchmark — 2026 pricing: $0, no per-message charge.] **WhatsApp Cloud API** is per-conversation (~$0.005–0.137; service replies free) **and** requires Meta Business verification + template approval (can take weeks). [benchmark] → **Build ChatOps on Telegram first**, add WhatsApp only if users demand it.
- **The advertising/partnership economics (honest):** you can't charge a provider for advertising until you have an **audience** — and pre-launch reach is ~0. The realistic sequence is the reverse of "charge them now": **(1)** use the free API to ship the feature and build users → **(2)** once you have measurable, engaged reach, sponsorship/affiliate becomes possible. Reference points: newsletter sponsorships run **~$20–50 CPM** (≈$200–500 per send at 10K subscribers); dev-tool affiliate/referral deals are common. **You typically need low-thousands of engaged users before a partner pays you.** So the "charge them for advertising" idea is right in spirit but **wrong in timing** — it's a later-stage lever, not a launch one.
