## 2 · The category splits into two philosophies — and a 2×2

- **Autonomy camp** — "runs while you sleep," speed over gates, live feeds, budget caps: **Polsia, NanoCorp, HeyBoss/Tycoon.**
- **Control camp** — human-in-the-loop approval, departments, managed: **cofounder.co** (and us).

Cross that with **opaque ↔ transparent**:

|  | Opaque | Transparent (live proof) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Autonomy** | — | **Polsia, NanoCorp** (live feeds) |
| **Control** | **cofounder.co** (no feed, no public pricing) | **← competitor.inc's open square** |

**The white space:** *control **and** transparency **and** validate-first **and** 0% cut.* Polsia/NanoCorp are transparent but autonomous (the trust risk). cofounder.co is controlled but **opaque** (no live feed, hidden pricing, gates that "slow the loop"). **No one occupies transparent-control + validate-first + own-your-upside.** That's our square.

## 3 · Why Polsia broke out (and the rest are "still building")

Through **Crossing the Chasm** + distribution + **7 Powers**:

1. **Distribution as the product.** Aggressive build-in-public spectacle (solo founder, "$100M ARR in 3 months," "the AI raised its own round") manufactured attention → signups. It out-*marketed* everyone, not out-*built*.
2. **Real execution, early.** It actually shipped end-to-end (code, ads, Stripe) while most rivals demoed — the first credible "it really runs a company" proof.
3. **Capital + nerve.** Raised $30M fast, deployed hard, **charged from day one** (the 20% cut funds the machine).
4. **First to cross the chasm** — mainstream attention before anyone else.

**But its "win" is fragile (the 7 Powers test).** Polsia has **brand/first-mover momentum**, not a durable moat: the 20% tax breeds resentment and the **2.1/5 trust score** ("marked complete without deploying," "credits burned") is a structural crack from speed-over-proof. Momentum ≠ power.

**Why the rest are still building:** the control/cautious players (cofounder.co's gates, NanoCorp's caps) are *better-behaved* but have **no distribution engine and little/no public revenue** — good products, no megaphone. In this category, **the megaphone won, not the architecture.**

## 4 · Porter's Five Forces — is this industry winnable?

- **Rivalry: HIGH** — many funded entrants, near-identical pitches, a crowded "cofounder/AI-company" naming swamp.
- **New entrants: HIGH** — frontier models commoditized the "brain"; barrier to a demo is a weekend.
- **Supplier power: HIGH & weird** — everyone rents intelligence from **Anthropic/OpenAI**, and **OpenAI's fund is backing several rivals** (HeyBoss/Tycoon) — the supplier is also a kingmaker.
- **Buyer power: HIGH** — low switching cost, many options, and buyers are **trust-sensitive** (Polsia's reviews prove it).
- **Substitutes: HIGH** — hire humans, use no-code, or DIY with raw Claude/Code.

**Read:** brutal structurally. You do **not** win by out-featuring funded incumbents. You win on **trust + a sharp niche + a distribution wedge** — the three things commoditized models *can't* hand your rivals.

## 5 · Where competitor.inc stands (the honest verdict)

**Position:** we own the unclaimed square — **transparent + human-controlled + validate-first + 0% cut** — which is a real, defensible counter-position none of them fully hold.

**The brutal truth (validate-first, applied to ourselves):**
- We are **pre-launch, $0 revenue, $100 budget, no funding** — against rivals with $3.5M–$30M and real traction.
- Our *original* wedge ("the honest, human-gated one vs. Polsia") is **no longer unique** — **cofounder.co already occupies the control/gated lane** (NY, "General Intelligence Company," departments + approval gates). We can't win on "we have approval gates" alone anymore.

**So the sharpened wedge (what's still ours alone):**
1. **Radical transparency** — the Glass Box + a *public* `/live` board with **verifiable** proof. cofounder.co is opaque (no feed, hidden pricing); we out-open them.
2. **Validate-before-build** — we'll tell you *not* to build. Nobody else leads with this.
3. **0% revenue share — you keep 100%.** Direct counter to Polsia's 20% tax; the funded players can't easily copy it (it's their revenue model).
4. **Niche down** — don't fight "build any company" head-on (where the giants spend). Pick a beachhead (e.g. indie/solo operators burned by the autonomous tools or the 20% tax) and own it.

**Scorecard vs the field**

**Bottom line:** the product position is genuinely strong and uncopyable; the **gap is distribution + proof of traction**, which is exactly what decided this market. Per the playbooks: **win on trust + a niche + a surprise-launch megaphone — not on features.** That's the plan we already have; this analysis says *sharpen the niche and the launch, and lean even harder on transparency + 0% cut,* because "human-gated" alone is now table stakes.
