**What does it do (problem → solution):**
First-time founders do it backwards — they fall in love with an idea, disappear for months to build it, launch
to silence, and only then learn nobody wanted it. competitor.inc inverts that: an AI crew runs an honest
demand test first and tells you the truth (even when that's "don't build this"), then — only on your approval —
builds, ships, and operates the winner. Every action is shown in a **Glass Box** with one-click undo, and
nothing consequential (spending money, posting, deploying) happens without your sign-off via an **Approval Inbox**.

**Why now:** Agentic AI just became capable enough to *do* the work of a startup's early functions, but the
funded incumbents (e.g. Polsia) spend users' money behind a curtain, take a revenue cut, and act before they
ask — and their own users say so. That's not a feature gap; it's a **trust gap**, and trust is a wedge a funded
incumbent can't copy without breaking their own model.

**Who it's for (beachhead):** first-time / student / new-grad founders — a *who*, not a vertical — so the engine
generates a **bespoke specialist crew per company** and can build/run any kind of company inside that niche.

**Stage & traction (honest):** Pre-launch. The full product is built and QA-green — validation gate, agent
crew, Glass Box, Approval Inbox, real demand-test instrumentation, gated execution (GitHub/email/deploy/

**What makes you win (the wedge):** counter-positioning on **honesty** — proof-first (Glass Box), human-in-
control (Approval Inbox), **0% revenue share**, validate-before-build. It lands hardest on first-timers, who
are most afraid of being fleeced or losing control.
