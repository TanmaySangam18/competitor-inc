# competitor.inc — user-research plan (ICP: first-time founders)

> Built by applying the (MIT-licensed) Cookiy user-research methodology — constructivist qual planning +
> the Sean-Ellis PMF survey. Purpose: replace the GROWTH-MODEL.md *assumptions* with real first-party data.
> Free methodology only; no paid/synthetic-participant dependency. Synthetic users are NOT used for
> validation (that would undercut our honest, real-demand-test wedge).

## The Big Q (the one thing we need to learn)
**"How do first-time / student founders decide whether to build a startup idea — and what makes them trust
or distrust a tool that tells them *not* to?"**
- Specific enough to answer; open enough for surprises. (Avoids the weak "what do users think of us?")

## The decision this research informs (no decision → don't run it)
1. The **landing/hero message** + which fear to lead with (reduce-fear playbook).
2. The **Validation-Gate framing** — does an honest "don't build" read as trustworthy or demotivating?
3. **Pricing/packaging** signal (does 0% revenue share / no-lock-in actually move them?).

## Method & sample (grounded in real norms)
- **Semi-structured interviews** (most versatile for mental models + decision-making) — **8–12 participants**
  reach thematic saturation; beyond ~15, returns diminish. [UX-research norm]
- Plus the **Sean-Ellis PMF survey** (quant) once there are ≥40 users who've run a validation. [PMF test]
- Representativeness > volume: 8 *real* first-time founders beat 1,000 irrelevant respondents.

## Screening questionnaire (recruit the right people)
1. Have you ever started — or seriously tried to start — a company or product? *(Yes, shipped / Yes, never shipped / No, but want to)* → keep "never shipped" + "want to."
2. Is this your **first** company? *(Yes / No)* → keep **Yes**.
3. Are you a student / recent grad? *(Yes / No)* → flag for the campus segment.
4. In the last 12 months, did you have an idea you didn't build? *(Yes / No)* → keep **Yes** (live pain).
5. Would you spend your own money to find out if an idea is worth building? *(Yes / Maybe / No)* → tells us willingness-to-pay.

## Interview guide
**Little q (the experience-near opener):** *"Tell me about the last startup or product idea you had —
walk me through what you did with it, starting wherever feels natural."*
Then, non-leading probes (let them lead; never ask "wouldn't it be great if…"):
- **The decision:** "How did you decide whether to actually build it? Walk me through that moment."
- **The fear:** "What worried you most about going ahead?" → listen for wasted-time / wasted-money / being-wrong.
- **Prior tools:** "Have you used any AI tool that acts on its own — spends, posts, builds? How did that feel?"
- **Control:** "How do you feel about a tool that does the work but asks before anything that costs money or goes public?"
- **The honest no:** "If a tool told you, with evidence, *don't build this yet* — would that help you or annoy you? Why?"
- **0% / lock-in:** "Does it matter to you whether a tool takes a cut of your revenue, or whether you can leave with your data?"
- Close: "What would have to be true for you to trust something like this with your first company?"

## Sean-Ellis PMF survey (the 40% test)
Ask after a user has run ≥1 validation:
1. **How would you feel if you could no longer use competitor.inc?** *(Very disappointed / Somewhat disappointed / Not disappointed / N/A — no longer use it)*
2. What type of person do you think would benefit most from competitor.inc?
3. What is the **main benefit** you get from it?
4. How can we improve it for you?
> **Threshold:** ≥**40%** answering "very disappointed" = a real PMF signal → pursue. <40% = iterate the product or the segment. (This is the KPI shown on `/house/board`.)

## Synthesis (when transcripts/notes exist)
Run the 5-phase thematic analysis: (1) familiarization → (2) coding → (3) themes → (4) synthesis → (5) report.
Output: the top 3–5 themes, each with verbatim quotes + the decision it changes.
